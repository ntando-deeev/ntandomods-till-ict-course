## COURSE OVERVIEW FOR TRAINER

**Your goal:** Turn a complete beginner into a job-ready cashier in 10 days. Every minute counts.

**What students fear most:** Making mistakes with money. Your job is to make them confident through repetition — not just theory.

---

## DAY-BY-DAY TRAINER NOTES

### Day 1 — Intro to Retail (Module 1)

**Trainer Preparation:**
- Print 10 "role play customer" cards with different shopping scenarios
- Prepare a list of 10 Zimbabwean retail shops as examples
- Have pens and notebooks ready for all students

**Key Points to Emphasise:**
- The retail sector employs thousands in Zimbabwe — this skill opens real doors
- Cashiers are the FACE of the business — first and last impression
- Integrity is non-negotiable — make this clear from day one

**Common Student Questions:**
- Q: "Do I need O-Levels?" A: No. NtandoMods is open to everyone.
- Q: "Will I get a job after this?" A: Retail employers actively look for certified cashiers.

**Role Play Tips:**
- Pair confident students with shy ones
- Model the greeting yourself first so students know what "good" looks like
- Give positive feedback loudly, correction feedback quietly

---

### Day 2 — POS Systems (Module 2)

**Trainer Preparation:**
- If you have a practice POS — set it up the night before
- If no POS: print the POS diagram from the module and laminate it
- Prepare 20 product cards with barcodes (can be hand-drawn)

**Teaching the POS Without Real Hardware:**
Use this sequence:
1. Show diagram of POS components — students label their own copy
2. Demonstrate login procedure verbally (or on a laptop simulation)
3. Do a "barcode scan" role play: one student is the "scanner" (calls out price), one is the cashier (types it)

**Common Mistakes to Watch For:**
- Students confusing void and refund — address this today, not on Day 3
- Students not knowing where to place their fingers on keypad — show them

---

### Day 3 — Transactions (Module 3)

**Trainer Preparation:**
- Prepare cash (real or printed fake notes) for change-giving practice
- Print receipt templates
- Write 5 transaction scenarios on slips of paper

**The Change-Giving Drill (most important exercise of the week):**
Run this drill 20+ times until every student can give change without hesitation:
1. Trainer announces: "Total is $7.40. Customer pays $10."
2. Student counts out change: "Here is $2.60 — $2 and 60 cents."
3. Trainer varies amounts until students stop counting on fingers

**Trainer Tip:** Students who struggle with arithmetic need extra practice. Pair them with a strong student for the change-giving drill.

---

### Day 4 — Cash Handling (Module 4)

**Trainer Preparation:**
- Prepare envelopes with mixed notes/coins for counting exercises
- Print Z-Read report templates
- Print cash-up sheets

**The Counting Race (engagement activity):**
Give each student an envelope with the same mixed amount. First student to count it correctly AND record it on the cash-up sheet wins. This builds speed under pressure.

**Teaching Fake Note Detection:**
If you don't have a UV light — teach the feel and visual test instead:
- Pass around a genuine $1 or $5 USD note
- Ask students: "What do you notice about the texture? The watermark?"
- Emphasise: when in doubt, call the supervisor — don't accuse the customer

---

### Day 5 — Customer Service & Security (Module 5)

**Trainer Preparation:**
- Prepare 4 "difficult customer" scenario cards
- Optional: film one role play to show students what professional looks like

**The Difficult Customer Role Play (key exercise):**
This must feel real. Trainer should play the difficult customer — be genuinely frustrated. Students must handle it professionally. Debrief after each:
- What did they do well?
- What could they improve?
- How did it feel? (normalise that it's uncomfortable — real life is too)

**Week 1 Summary:**
- Do a 15-question verbal quiz at end of Day 5 covering the full week
- Students who cannot answer basic change-giving or void/refund questions need weekend practice

### Days 6–9 — ICT Basics (Modules 6–9)

**Essential Rule for ICT Teaching:**
NEVER talk about computers for more than 15 minutes without having students on the computers doing something. ICT is learned by doing.

**If Computers Are Limited (fewer computers than students):**
- Pair students (one types, one guides — swap every 20 minutes)
- Use printed keyboard diagrams for finger placement practice
- Use phone (Android) as a substitute for Excel/Word practice if needed

**Typing Practice Schedule:**
- 15 minutes of typing practice at the START of every ICT day
- Use online typing tools if internet is available (typing.com, keybr.com)
- Track each student's WPM daily and show them their improvement

**Excel — The #1 Struggle Point:**
Most students will struggle with formulas. Use this sequence:
1. First teach with calculator: "3+3 = 6. Excel does the same thing."
2. Then show: =3+3 in a cell. Press enter. Magic.
3. Then cell references: =A1+A2. Move to =SUM(A1:A5).
4. NEVER skip ahead — wait until everyone understands each step.

### Day 10 — Assessment Day (Module 10)

**Morning Routine:**
- 1-hour revision session — students ask questions, you clarify
- Run through the most common exam questions verbally
- Remind students: read every question carefully. Don't rush.

**During the Written Exam:**
- Walk the room every 10 minutes
- If a student is stuck on a question — remind them to move on and come back
- Keep exact time — start and stop on the dot

**Practical Assessment:**
- Set up the till area before students arrive
- Have all scenario cards ready
- Mark each student on the rubric as they go — don't rely on memory
- Give brief verbal feedback immediately after each practical

**Certificate Day:**
- Print certificates the night before
- Write each student's name neatly (or print)
- Call each student by name to receive their certificate
- Photograph the moment (with permission)

## FREQUENTLY ASKED TRAINER QUESTIONS

**Q: What if a student fails?**
A: They get one free rewrite within 30 days. If they fail the rewrite, they must re-enrol.

**Q: What if I don't have a real POS machine?**
A: Use the printed simulation in the course materials. Many employers will train on their specific system after hiring — your job is to teach the concepts and confidence.

**Q: What if students don't speak English well?**
A: Teach in English but allow Shona/Ndebele explanations for complex concepts. The workplace will require English, so build that habit gently.

**Q: A student is clearly struggling with the course. What do I do?**
A: Give them extra one-on-one time during breaks. If they are genuinely unable to keep up, speak to management — they may need to repeat certain modules.
