## SECTION 1 — WHO IS A NTANDOMODS TRAINER?

A NtandoMods trainer is patient, practical, and passionate about lifting Zimbabweans out of unemployment through real skills. You are not just a teacher — you are a career changer.

### Trainer Requirements
- Completed NtandoMods course (or equivalent experience)
- Patience and strong communication skills
- Basic computer proficiency (Word, Excel, internet)
- Willingness to learn and update knowledge

### Trainer Code of Conduct
- Arrive 15 minutes before class every day
- Never cancel a class without 24-hour notice to students
- Treat every student equally regardless of age, background, or ability
- Never accept gifts or favours from students in exchange for marks
- Report any concerns about students to management
- Maintain student records confidentially

## SECTION 2 — LESSON PLANNING

### The NtandoMods 4-Part Lesson Structure

Every lesson must follow this structure:

| Part | Time | Purpose |
|------|------|---------|
| **1. Warm-Up** | 10 mins | Review yesterday's content with 3 quick questions |
| **2. Teach** | 90 mins | New content delivery |
| **3. Practice** | 60 mins | Students practice the new skill |
| **4. Check** | 20 mins | Quiz or verbal check of understanding |

## SECTION 3 — TEACHING METHODS

### Method 1: Demonstration First
Show students how to do something before asking them to try it.
> "Watch me do this first, then you try."

### Method 2: Think-Pair-Share
Ask a question. Students think alone (1 min), discuss with partner (2 mins), share with class.

### Method 3: Role Play
Essential for customer service, cashiering, and communication skills. Rotate roles so everyone practices both sides.

### Method 4: Peer Teaching
Once a student masters something, ask them to teach a struggling classmate. This reinforces both students' learning.

### Method 5: Real-World Examples
Always use Zimbabwean examples: OK Supermarket, EcoCash, TM Pick n Pay, Choppies. Never use abstract foreign examples.

## SECTION 4 — MANAGING A CLASSROOM

### Classroom Setup
- Arrange desks in U-shape or rows facing the screen/board
- Ensure every student can see demonstrations clearly
- Keep the training area tidy and organised
- Post the weekly timetable visibly on the wall

### Managing Different Learning Levels
Some students will be fast learners, others will need more time. This is normal.

**For fast learners:**
- Give extension tasks (more complex versions of the exercise)
- Ask them to assist slower learners (with your supervision)
- Give them leadership roles in group activities

**For slow learners:**
- Never shame them in front of the class
- Spend additional time with them during practice sessions
- Break tasks into smaller steps
- Celebrate every small improvement

### Handling Phones in Class
- Phones must be on silent during lessons
- Phones are ONLY allowed for WhatsApp Business lessons
- Students caught using phones for personal reasons lose phone privileges that day

## SECTION 5 — ASSESSMENT & GRADING

### Grading Scale
| % | Grade | Description |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 90–100 | A+ | Distinction |
| 80–89 | A | Merit |
| 70–79 | B | Credit |
| 60–69 | C | Pass |
| Below 60 | F | Fail — must rewrite |

### Rules for Running Examinations
1. Read all exam instructions aloud before starting
2. Ensure no student can see another's paper
3. No talking after exam begins
4. If a student is caught cheating — remove paper, record incident, report to management
5. Stay in the room for the entire exam duration
6. Collect ALL papers before students leave

### Marking Guidelines
- Mark consistently — use the marking guide for every paper
- Write brief feedback on each paper (not just a score)
- Return papers within 3 working days
- Keep all marked papers on file for 1 year

## SECTION 6 — STUDENT WELFARE

### Signs a Student May Be Struggling
- Missing classes frequently
- Not participating in activities
- Falling asleep in class
- Submitting blank or incomplete assessments

### What To Do
1. Speak to the student privately — never in front of class
2. Ask open questions: "How are you finding the course? Is anything making it hard to attend?"
3. Offer solutions: catch-up sessions, payment plans, flexible timetables
4. Report serious concerns to management

### Creating an Inclusive Environment
- Use simple English — many students are not first-language speakers
- Allow questions in Shona or Ndebele if students struggle to express themselves in English
- Avoid jargon — always explain technical terms
- Celebrate diversity — students come from different backgrounds

## SECTION 7 — RECORD KEEPING

### Daily Register
Take attendance every day. Use the standard register sheet. Students who miss more than 20% of classes cannot sit the exam without management approval.

### Trainer's Daily Log
After each day, write 3 sentences:
1. What did I teach today?
2. What did students find difficult?
3. What will I do differently tomorrow?

### Assessment Records
Keep a mark sheet for every student, every assessment. Submit copies to management at end of each month.

## SECTION 8 — CERTIFICATES & GRADUATION

### Who Gets a Certificate
Only students who:
- Attended at least 80% of classes
- Passed both written exam (60%+) AND practical assessment (60%+)

### The Ceremony
Even for small groups — hold a simple certificate ceremony. It matters enormously to students. Invite family members if possible. Take photos (with permission).
