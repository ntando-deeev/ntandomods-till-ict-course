## 📖 Lesson 5.1 — Customer Service Excellence (60 minutes)

### The GREAT Customer Service Framework
| Letter | Stands For | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **G** | Greet | Always greet first, with a smile |
| **R** | Respond | Listen and respond promptly |
| **E** | Empathise | Understand how the customer feels |
| **A** | Act | Solve the problem or escalate |
| **T** | Thank | Always thank the customer |

### Phrases That Build Trust
- "Good morning, how can I help you today?"
- "I understand that must be frustrating."
- "Let me check that for you right away."
- "Thank you for your patience."
- "Have a wonderful day!"

### Phrases to AVOID
- "I don't know" (say "Let me find out for you")
- "That's not my department" (help them find the right person)
- "It's not my fault" (even if true — not helpful)
- "You'll have to wait" (say "I'll be with you in just a moment")

## 📖 Lesson 5.2 — Handling Difficult Customers (45 minutes)

### Types of Difficult Customers

**1. The Angry Customer**
- Stay calm. Their anger is about the situation, not you personally.
- Lower your voice — it encourages them to lower theirs.
- Say: "I hear you and I want to help. Let me see what I can do."

**2. The Impatient Customer**
- Acknowledge the wait: "Thank you for your patience, I'll be right with you."
- Move efficiently but accurately — don't rush and make errors.

**3. The Confused Customer**
- Be patient and explain clearly.
- Offer to show them rather than just tell them.

**4. The Dishonest Customer**
- Stay calm and professional.
- Do not accuse — call a supervisor.
- Document everything.

### The 3-Step De-escalation Technique
1. **Listen** — let them finish speaking without interrupting
2. **Acknowledge** — show you understand how they feel
3. **Solve** — offer a solution or get someone who can

## 📖 Lesson 5.3 — Retail Security & Loss Prevention (45 minutes)

### Common Types of Retail Theft in Zimbabwe
1. **Shoplifting** — hiding items in bags, pockets, or under clothing
2. **Sweethearting** — cashier does not scan items for friends/family
3. **Barcode swapping** — customer puts cheap barcode over expensive item
4. **Under-ringing** — cashier intentionally enters lower price
5. **Refund fraud** — returning stolen or used items for cash
6. **Short-change fraud** — customer confuses cashier during change

### How to Protect Yourself and the Business
- Always scan EVERY item — no exceptions for friends or family
- Count change carefully and announce it: "Here is your $6.50 change"
- If something feels wrong, call a supervisor — trust your instincts
- Be aware of customers who distract you while others access the till
- Never allow anyone behind your till without permission

### Sweethearting — The Cashier's Own Risk
> Sweethearting (not scanning items for someone you know) is theft. You can be fired AND prosecuted under Zimbabwean law. It is not worth it. Management has cameras. The POS system tracks every item.

## 📖 Lesson 5.4 — End-of-Day Procedures (45 minutes)

### Checklist — Before You Leave Your Till
- [ ] Run Z-Read report and print copy
- [ ] Complete cash-up form
- [ ] Count float separately and return to supervisor
- [ ] Remove all personal items from till area
- [ ] Log out of POS system
- [ ] Switch off receipt printer and scanner
- [ ] Wipe down the till area
- [ ] Report any issues or unusual events to supervisor
- [ ] Sign off in the daily shift register
